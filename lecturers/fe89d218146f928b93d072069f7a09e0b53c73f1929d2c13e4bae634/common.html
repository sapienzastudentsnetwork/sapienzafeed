Il corso è focalizzato principalmente sulla programmazione di sistema per sistemi Unix/Linux.

L'obiettivo è rendere gli studenti capaci di comprendere, scrivere e modificare programmi che si interfacciano con il sistema operativo Linux e con il codice sorgente del suo kernel.

Più precisamente, ci si aspetta che gli studenti acquisiscano familiarità con:

  • Il linguaggio C

     e gli strumenti normalmente utilizzati nell'ambiente di sviluppo (compilatore, preprocessore, debugger, make, shell, ecc.).
  • Le funzioni fondamentali

     del sistema operativo e i suoi moduli principali (Scheduler, Gestore della Memoria Virtuale, Filesystem...).
  • Le principali primitive di sistema

     per la creazione e la sincronizzazione dei processi, lo scambio di messaggi e di informazioni (questo costituisce il nucleo del corso).
  • Le primitive per la programmazione di rete

     (socket).

E che siano successivamente in grado di:

  • Utilizzare le primitive

     fornite dal sistema operativo e integrarle correttamente nel codice.
  • Scegliere la componente

     e le funzioni del sistema operativo più adatte, in base alle esigenze delle applicazioni e alla loro modalità di esecuzione.
  • Determinare la complessità

     e le modalità di implementazione di un'applicazione di sistema.
  • Descrivere l'interazione

     di un'applicazione con il sistema operativo e spiegare le ragioni alla base delle scelte effettuate.
  • Continuare l'apprendimento

     esaminando, nel dettaglio, l'architettura e l'interfaccia di programmazione del sistema operativo.

In the classroom before or after classes, or by appointment

In November 1985, I graduated with honors (110/110) in Electronic Engineering from the First University of Rome (La Sapienza), under the supervision of Prof. Carlo Batini; my thesis title was: "Algorithm Design for the Automatic Layout of Hierarchical Graphs".
In 1986, I was awarded a scholarship, also at the Institute of Computer Science of the University of Rome, to develop a prototype for an automatic Entity/Relationship diagram plotter ("Giotto").
After completing my military service, I started working at Tecsiel (IRI-Finsiel Group) on the "Dafne Tools" project, aimed at developing a suite of tools to automate the software production methodology, Dafne, used within the Finsiel group.
Subsequently, in 1988, I joined the European Center for Scientific and Engineering Computing (IBM ECSEC), where my main focus was on developing cooperative processing prototypes, parallel programming libraries, and High-Performance Computing (HPC) in general.
For two of these prototypes, I received "Outstanding Technical Achievement" awards in 1993 and 1995. The first was for cooplib, a library designed to facilitate cooperative application development between mainframes and workstations, while the second was related to PVMe, an implementation of the public domain PVM (Parallel Virtual Machine) library, specifically optimized for IBM 9076 SP1 and SP2 systems. PVMe was offered as an IBM product from 1995 to 1998.
During this period, I also taught numerous internal and external courses on parallel programming in distributed environments and, in 1994, I chaired the first PVM European User Group Meeting.
Consequently, I also spent several extended periods in the United States at the IBM laboratories in Kingston, Poughkeepsie, and Yorktown Heights.
Then, in 1995, I joined Silicon Graphics (SGI), where I continued to work on issues related to intensive numerical and parallel computing as a pre-sales systems engineer.
Starting from 1997, I moved to SUN Microsystems, where, still as a pre-sales systems engineer, I served as the Italian point of reference for High-Performance Computing (HPC).
During these years, I gained significant experience in code parallelization and optimization, particularly in C, C++, FORTRAN 77, and FORTRAN 90, across both shared and distributed memory environments.
Finally, in the spring of 2000, I returned to IBM Italy within the "System & Technology Group", where I initially continued my pre-sales activities for Unix systems (pSeries).
In January 2004, I stepped into the role of “Systems Architect”, providing consulting services for the design of complex I/T infrastructures involving heterogeneous hardware and software components.
From 2004 to 2007, I participated in numerous complex environment projects for online trading, home banking, and e-commerce applications. In these projects, I leveraged my prior experience in performance evaluation and heterogeneous distributed processing systems.
In 2007, I joined the IBM TEC (Technical Expert Council), a team of technical leaders from various IBM divisions (hardware, software, services, and laboratories), while also returning to work in the field of High-Performance Computing (HPC).
Over the last two years, I have returned to focusing on high-performance numerical computing (HPC), Deep Learning, and I/T architecture optimization.
Throughout my career, I have also maintained strong ties with the academic world. In this capacity, I have taught as an adjunct professor:

• The 2nd level Master's program in “IT Governance”, organized by the Faculty of Engineering at Roma Tre University in collaboration with IBM Italy and FORMIT Servizi (2008, 2010, and 2012), where I taught a course on computing system architectures.
